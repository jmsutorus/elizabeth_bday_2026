The Elizabethan age

On the ninth day of December, in the year 1996, the realm received its finest monarch. Three decades on, her reign has been marked by great laughter, good counsel, and an unbroken record of being right about most things. A thirtieth year is a pearl jubilee, and a jubilee demands a faire.

What awaits thee

Knights, clashing

Jousts and sword fights, fought by professionals so that we need not. Cheer loudly and pick a favourite.

Minstrels, minstreling

Live music, wandering players and shows all day, plus stalls of artisan wares for the weak of will.

Feasting and frolic

Turkey legs the size of thy forearm, a cup of something festive, and dancing for those so moved.

Counsel for travellers

  • Garb is heartily encouraged and never required. Knights, fairies, pirates, wizards and people in jeans are all welcome at court.
  • The faire is out of doors. December in Texas is fickle, so wear layers under thy cloak and shoes fit for walking.
  • Bring coin for food, drink and trinkets.

Claim thy faire name

No one enters the queen's court as a commoner. Tell the royal scribe thy name and receive a title to bear on the day.

Thy title shall appear here.

Send word

Pray tell Joseph whether thou shalt attend, that we may know how many turkey legs to brace for. A text, a raven or a well-trained falcon will all do nicely.

Long may she reign.
